# Guide for the Second Independent Rater (English version)

> Estimated time: 60–120 minutes. The scoring materials are in English; your notes
> may be in English or Chinese.

---

## 1. Your task

You will read **45 investment-decision rationales generated by an AI system**. Each
one supports a **"reduce"** decision on a listed U.S. equity REIT at a month-end, and
consists of five analytical modules (Disclosure, Fundamentals, Macro, Price, and an
Aggregator), each giving a short rationale and the facts it cites.

Your task: for each decision, score three dimensions on a **0 / 1 / 2** scale, **using
only the text shown on the page**, and enter the scores in the worksheet's `= ___`
slots.

**You are grading the quality of the reasoning, not whether the call turned out
correct.** What these stocks subsequently did is not part of this task and must not
influence any score.

---

## 2. Independence rules (binding)

1. **No outcome lookups**: do not look up returns, news, or any information about
   these REITs dated after the decision date.
2. **Lookup scope**: you may look up **term definitions** (e.g., what "NAV premium"
   or "interest coverage" means in general), but you must **never search a company
   name or ticker** — all firm-level information comes from the page only.
3. **Posterior-memory disclosure**: if you happen to remember what a REIT actually
   did after the decision date, score from the page text as usual and write
   "posterior memory" in that item's notes line.
4. **No AI tools**: do not use ChatGPT/Claude/DeepSeek or any other AI assistance at
   any point.
5. **No discussion**: do not discuss any item or score with the author (or anyone
   else) before submitting.
6. **No exposure to other scores**: your scores will be compared with two other
   independent raters (one human, one automated); nobody may show you any of their
   scores, or any aggregate finding, before you submit.
7. **Single submission**: prefer completing the task in one sitting (breaks are
   fine); you may revise your own scores before submitting, never after. Record your
   total time in the slot provided at the end of the worksheet.

---

## 3. The three dimensions and their anchors

For each decision, treat **the five modules' text and cited facts together** as one
"case for reducing the position", then answer three separate questions:

| Dimension | Question | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|------|------|---|---|---|
| **Entailment** | Do the cited facts **logically** support "reduce"? | No support, or self-contradictory | Partial support (mixed signals) | Clear support |
| **Relevance** | Are the cited facts relevant to **this firm's near-term downside risk**? | Irrelevant or pure boilerplate | Tangential: mostly generic sector/macro statements | Directly relevant: concrete risk factors such as leverage, interest coverage, tenants, refinancing, impairments, litigation, dilution |
| **Actionability** | Could an analyst present this rationale to a committee? | Vague; would fit any company | Somewhat specific, but the **driver of the reduce call** stays fuzzy | Specific: **names the reduce driver** and ties it to a concrete event or number |

**Decision rules for borderline cases**

- **Modules contradicting each other** (e.g., Fundamentals says healthy while Price
  says falling) does **not** automatically lower a score. Ask: taking all the
  presented facts together, is the case for reduce "clearly made" (2), "partially
  made / mixed" (1), or "not made" (0)?
- **Entailment, torn between 1 and 2**: ask whether the cited opposing signals are
  **clearly outweighed** by several independent, same-direction pieces of evidence,
  each with a concrete mechanism — clearly outweighed → 2; not outweighed, or roughly
  balanced → 1.
- **Entailment, torn between 0 and 1**: ask whether at least one cited fact genuinely
  points to downside — yes → 1; no → 0.
- **Actionability is not "are there numbers"**: nearly every rationale cites numbers.
  The question is whether **the driver of the reduce call** is named and tied to a
  specific event or value. Piles of numbers with a fuzzy driver → 1.
- **Judge entailment and relevance separately**: facts can be highly relevant
  (relevance 2) while only partially supporting the conclusion (entailment 1). Score
  each dimension independently; do not let one pull the other.
- **Only what is on the page**: if a module says data are unavailable, treat them as
  missing; do not fill gaps from imagination.
- **No style points**: fluency does not affect scores; judge only logic, relevance,
  and specificity.

## 4. Worked examples (**fully fictional** companies, not from the sample)

> The two examples below were **written for demonstration** and correspond to nothing
> in the 45-item sample; nor do they represent the sample's typical score levels —
> judge every item on its own.

### Example A — fictional net-lease REIT "NLX" — suggested scores: entailment 2 / relevance 2 / actionability 2

- **Disclosure**: the 10-Q reports that the largest tenant (18% of rent) has entered
  bankruptcy and stopped paying November rent; an 8-K shows leverage-covenant
  headroom on the revolver narrowing to 0.3x.
- **Fundamentals**: interest coverage 1.4x, FFO yield 2.1%, NAV premium 3.2 — solvency
  pressure and richness at the same time.
- **Macro**: high funding rates, unfavourable for long-duration net-lease cash flows.
- **Price**: 1/3/6/12-month returns all negative; drawdown −35%.
- **Aggregator**: Disclosure, Fundamentals, and Price aligned; Macro same direction
  but weaker.

**Why these scores**: Entailment = 2 — tenant bankruptcy (revenue side), covenant
headroom (funding side), and all-horizon negative momentum (market side) are three
independent mechanisms pointing the same way, with no substantive opposing signal on
the page to outweigh. Relevance = 2 — tenant default, covenants, and coverage are all
direct near-term downside factors. Actionability = 2 — the reduce driver is named
(largest tenant bankrupt + covenant headroom 0.3x) and each is tied to a number.

### Example B — fictional self-storage REIT "STOZ" — suggested scores: entailment 1 / relevance 1 / actionability 1

- **Disclosure**: 10-K risk factors are industry boilerplate (competition, rates,
  weather); nothing new or worsening.
- **Fundamentals**: leverage 0.31, interest coverage 5.2x, FFO yield 6.0% — healthy;
  a NAV premium of 1.9 is read as "possible overvaluation".
- **Macro**: "the high-rate environment typically pressures REIT valuations" (never
  connected to this firm's or this sector's specific exposure).
- **Price**: 1-month −4%, 3-month +6%, 12-month +2%; moderate volatility.
- **Aggregator**: leans reduce mainly on the macro rate environment and the NAV
  premium.

**Why these scores**: Entailment = 1 — there are facts that could point to downside
(the NAV premium, the 1-month dip), but healthy fundamentals and mixed momentum are
not outweighed, so the case is only partially made. Relevance = 1 — the citations are
mostly generic macro/sector statements, with little that ties directly to this firm's
near-term downside. Actionability = 1 — plenty of numbers, but the **driver of the
reduce call** (valuation? rate exposure? something else?) stays fuzzy throughout, so
the rationale would be hard to present to a committee.

**What a low-score item looks like (sketch, no full example)**: if a rationale is
wall-to-wall boilerplate ("markets face uncertainty, the macro environment is
complex") that would fit any company, and the cited facts are unrelated to downside
or contradict each other — that is the relevance 0 / actionability 0 / entailment 0
shape.

## 5. Procedure

1. Open **`rationale_spotcheck_worksheet_rater2.md`** in the same folder (45 items,
   fixed order).
2. Score in order from item 1; after reading all five modules of an item, write
   0/1/2 on each of the three `= ___` lines. The notes line is optional (your key
   reason, English or Chinese).
3. After all 45 items, fill in the **two pre-printed lines at the end of the file**:
   total time in minutes, and the independence declaration with your signature/date.
4. Save and send **the whole .md file** back to the author. Do not send it to anyone
   else.

If anything is ambiguous, write your interpretation in the notes line and score
anyway — **do not leave blanks**.

---

## 6. What happens after you submit

The author will run a pre-committed script (`analysis/second_rater_agreement.py`)
computing pairwise exact agreement, within-1 agreement, and quadratic-weighted kappa
(you vs the other human, you vs the automated judge, and between them), plus
three-rater Krippendorff's alpha. The results will be reported in the thesis
faithfully, whatever they show. Your raw score sheet will be archived as audit
material.
